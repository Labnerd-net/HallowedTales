Nicholas of Flue, known in Switzerland as Brother Klaus, lived the first
fifty years of his life as a farmer, soldier, and local magistrate in the
canton of Obwalden. He was also a husband and the father of ten children.
In 1467, with his wife's consent, he left home to live as a hermit in the
Ranft gorge, a short walk from the family farm. There he prayed, received
visitors, and, by every account, ate nothing.

According to the tradition, Nicholas lived for nineteen or twenty years on
the Eucharist alone. The story spread quickly and was tested more than
once. Local authorities reportedly posted watchers around his cell to see
whether anyone brought him food, and a representative of the bishop came
to question him. Both the watchers and the bishop's man went away
satisfied. Pilgrims came from across Europe to see him, and the visitors'
accounts that survive from his own lifetime, including a merchant's
detailed description of the hermit, his family, and his manner, are among
the reasons his case is so often cited.

## The hermit at Stans

The part of Brother Klaus's story that the Swiss remember best has nothing
to do with food. In 1481 the Swiss Confederation stood on the edge of civil
war, as the city cantons and the rural cantons quarreled over how to share
the spoils of recent victories. A priest, Heini am Grund, is said to have
hurried to the Ranft to ask the hermit's advice, and to have carried back
a message to the assembly at Stans. Within hours the delegates reportedly
agreed to a settlement, which became known as the Covenant of Stans.

Nicholas is honored today as a patron saint of Switzerland, and his
prayer, "My Lord and my God, take from me everything that keeps me from
you," is still said by Swiss Catholics.

## A phenomenon with centuries of company

Nicholas stands in a long line of reported fasting saints, including
Catherine of Siena, the 14th-century Dominican who advised popes, Lidwina of
Schiedam, the Dutch mystic, and Rose of Lima, the first saint of the
Americas. He is remembered less for the fast than for what he did as a
peacemaker, in a country that very nearly went to war with itself.
