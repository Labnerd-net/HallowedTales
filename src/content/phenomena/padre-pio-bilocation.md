Padre Pio rarely left the friary at San Giovanni Rotondo in the decades he
spent there. And yet, by multiple separate accounts, people who should
have had no way of encountering him there reported seeing him - or being
helped by him - in places he never physically traveled to.

One of the most frequently told accounts comes from Italian general Luigi
Cadorna, who in 1917 reportedly encountered a friar matching Pio's
description on a battlefield, offering comfort to soldiers, though Pio was,
by every account of his daily life, in San Giovanni Rotondo at the time.
Decades later, during the Second World War, Allied bomber pilots preparing
to strike the area reportedly described seeing a friar's figure in the sky
ahead of their aircraft, and found themselves unable to drop their bombs on
the town.

## A phenomenon with centuries of company

Pio's case is the most recent and most widely told example of bilocation in
Catholic tradition, but the pattern stretches back much further. Martin de
Porres is said to have bilocated to assist the poor as far away as China,
Japan, and Africa. Francis Xavier reportedly preached from a ship while
somehow also preaching ashore. Anthony of Padua is remembered for
delivering a sermon in two locations at the same moment. Each case stands
on its own, but together they form one of the most consistently reported
phenomena across very different centuries of saintly life.
