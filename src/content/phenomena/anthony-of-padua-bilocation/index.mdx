Anthony was born in Lisbon around 1195, became a Franciscan, and spent his
short life preaching across Italy and France before dying near Padua at
about thirty-six. The city he is named for is not the city he came from,
and that distance sits at the heart of one of his best-loved stories.

By the tradition, word reached Anthony in Padua that his father back in
Lisbon had been accused of murder and was about to be hanged. Anthony,
reportedly, asked leave to go, then appeared in Lisbon in an instant,
though the journey would have taken weeks. In the best-known telling he
asked to see the dead man, who spoke and declared Anthony's father
innocent, and the father was freed. Anthony was then gone as suddenly as
he had arrived, and back in Padua before anyone there had noticed his
absence.

Portuguese painters returned to the scene again and again, and the
father-and-gallows picture became a familiar sight in churches and
monasteries across the country. For a saint who spent his life far from
home, it is a story about a son who found a way back when it mattered.

## Another place, another hour

A second bilocation story is told of Anthony in France. He was preaching
on Holy Thursday (or, in other versions, at Easter) when he remembered
that he was due to sing a lesson at the choir of his own monastery that
same hour. In the story, he pulled his hood over his face, and in the same
moment appeared among the brothers in the choir, sang his part, and then
vanished - while the congregation he had been addressing waited, unaware.

## A pattern with company

Anthony is far from the only saint of whom this is told. Francis Xavier is
said to have been seen preaching ashore while his ship was at sea, Martin
de Porres is said to have reached the poor in distant lands without leaving
his friary, and Padre Pio's reported appearances in the twentieth century
are among the most recent in a very long line. What stands out in
Anthony's case is how personal the stories are: a father, a choir, an
errand of love rather than a display of power.
