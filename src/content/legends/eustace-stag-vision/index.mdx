Eustace, a Roman general said to have lived under the Roman emperor
Trajan or Hadrian in the 2nd century, was out hunting one day when he
pursued a magnificent stag deep into the forest. The animal turned to
face him, and he saw a crucifix shining between its antlers. A voice
called out from the vision, identifying itself as Christ, and asked
Eustace why he was pursuing it rather than seeking it out directly.

Eustace converted on the spot and had himself and his entire household
baptized that same day, moved enough by the vision to walk away from the
life he had built as a general without a second thought. In the fuller
version of his legend, he went on to endure a string of dramatic
reversals of fortune that cost him his wealth, separated him from his
family for years, and eventually led to his martyrdom alongside his wife
and sons.

## The older half of a shared vision

Historians generally treat Eustace's story as legendary rather than
historical - no outside record confirms a Roman general of this name
living this life, and the earliest surviving text dates many centuries
after the events it claims to describe. The Church removed him from its
universal calendar in 1969 for that reason, though local devotion to him
has continued regardless, a sign of how much it was the vision itself,
rather than the biography built around it, that people kept returning
to. That staying power may be the most interesting thing about Eustace
today: centuries later, the same glowing stag reappeared, almost
unchanged, in the far better documented life of Saint Hubert, the bishop
of Liège who became the patron saint of hunters, told in full in Saint
Hubert and the Stag of the Vision.
