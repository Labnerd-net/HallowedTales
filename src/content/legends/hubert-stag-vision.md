Before he became a bishop in the Belgian city of Liege around the year
700, Hubert was, by every account, a nobleman wholly devoted to hunting,
to the point of neglecting his religious duties entirely. The turning point came on a Good Friday, of all days,
when Hubert rode into the forest of the Ardennes after a magnificent stag
while the rest of the faithful were at prayer.

As he closed in on the animal, it turned to face him, and Hubert saw a
crucifix glowing between its antlers. A voice - the story doesn't specify
whose, though the implication is unmistakable - asked him how much longer
he intended to hunt the forest instead of turning toward God. Hubert
dismounted on the spot, and the conversion that followed was total: he
gave up his title, took up religious life, and was eventually consecrated
bishop of Liege, where he spent the rest of his life evangelizing the
still largely pagan Ardennes region.

## A vision shared with another saint

Hubert's stag has become one of the most recognizable images connected to
any saint - it's the reason hunters, trappers, and furriers still claim
him as their patron. But almost exactly the same vision, down to the glowing
crucifix between the antlers, is told about a much earlier and far more
legendary figure, the Roman general-turned-martyr Eustace. Historians
generally regard the Eustace version as the older story and believe it
was later grafted onto Hubert, whose life as a real, historically
documented bishop gave the borrowed vision a more solid home to live in.
