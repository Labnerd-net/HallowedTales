This story comes from The Golden Legend, a 13th-century collection of
saints' lives that became one of the most widely read books of the
Middle Ages. According to that telling, a dragon had taken up residence
near the city of Silene, poisoning the surrounding countryside with its
breath and demanding a steady tribute of sheep to stay away from the
town. When the sheep ran out, the town turned to drawing lots among its
own children, and the lot eventually fell to the king's own daughter.

Into this scene rides George, a soldier and martyr of the early Church
who later became the patron saint of England. He happens upon the
princess waiting for the dragon, already resigned to her fate. He challenges the beast directly, wounds it with his lance,
then has the princess loop her girdle around its neck so it follows behind
her like a leashed dog, entirely subdued. George leads the dragon into the
city and offers its people a bargain: he will finish the creature off for
good, if they agree to be baptized. They agree, and by some tellings,
thousands are baptized that same day.

## A legend grafted onto an older saint

<InlineImage
  src={vatopediGeorgeIcon}
  alt="Byzantine ivory relief icon of Saint George standing in military dress holding a spear and shield, with no dragon present"
  caption="Saint George as standing warrior-martyr, 11th-century ivory relief, Vatopedi Monastery, Mount Athos"
  credit="Wikimedia Commons, public domain"

/>

George was already a venerated martyr for roughly a thousand years before
any surviving account mentions a dragon at all - early devotion to him
traces back to at least the 5th century in the Eastern Church, built
entirely around his martyrdom under persecution, with no monster in
sight. The dragon episode seems to have entered Western tradition later,
likely carried home by crusaders who had encountered some version of the
story in the East. It was The Golden Legend that locked the tale into
the form still told today - a clear example of a popular saint
accumulating new legendary material centuries after his own lifetime.
