Image research for the 8 monthly-devotion entries (see "Monthly devotions" section of
`Pillar 2 - Catholic Folk Traditions.md`). Each entry below shows the image actually wired into
the entry's frontmatter, followed by alternates that were sourced and approved-against but not
used - keep these in case we want to swap later. All candidates are public domain (artist died
100+ years ago) or explicitly CC0/CC-BY-SA; ambiguous-license candidates are flagged and were not
selected.

## January - Month of the Holy Name

**Used:** Sano di Pietro, "St. Bernardino Preaching in the Campo," 1445. PD-old-100.
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:St_Bernardino_preaching_in_the_Campo.Sano_di_Pietro.jpg

Alternates:
- Francisco Goya, "San Bernardino de Siena," 1784 (Bernardine preaching before King Alfonso V of
  Aragon; Goya reportedly included a self-portrait in the crowd). PD-old-100.
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:San_Bernardino_de_Siena_(Goya).jpg
- Daniel Hopfer, "IHS Monogram surrounded by Six Angels," etching c. 1485-1536. CC0 (Met Museum).
  Standalone-symbol option if a figurative painting isn't wanted.
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:IHS_Monogram_surrounded_by_Six_Angels_MET_DP828566.jpg

## March - Month of St. Joseph

**Used:** Guido Reni, "Saint Joseph and the Christ Child," c. 1640. PD-old-100.
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Guido_Reni_-_Saint_Joseph_and_the_Christ_Child_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg

Alternates:
- El Greco, "St Joseph and the Christ Child," c. 1600 (more austere Mannerist style). PD-old-100.
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:El_Greco_-_St_Joseph_and_the_Christ_Child_-_WGA10536.jpg
- Jusepe de Ribera, "Saint Joseph with the Flowering Rod," c. 1630 (Joseph alone with his
  patronage attribute, no Christ child - good if the "patron" angle should lead instead of the
  father-son intimacy angle). PD-old-100.
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Brooklyn_Museum_-_Saint_Joseph_with_the_Flowering_Rod_-_Jusepe_de_Ribera_-_overall.jpg
- Murillo, "Saint Joseph and the Christ Child," c. 1670-75. PD-old-100.
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Murillo_-_Saint_Joseph_and_the_Christ_Child,_ca._1670-75.jpg

## May - Month of Mary

**Used:** Stefan Lochner, "Madonna of the Rose Bush," c. 1440. PD-old-100.
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Stefan_Lochner_-_Madonna_of_the_Rose_Bush_-_WGA13346.jpg

Alternates:
- Botticelli, "Madonna of the Rose Garden," 1469-70. PD-old-100.
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:MadonnaRosetoBotticelli.jpg
- Rubens & Jan Brueghel the Elder, "Madonna in a Garland of Flowers," c. 1619. Flagged: Commons
  lists the digital reproduction as CC BY 4.0 rather than a PD-Art tag - double check before use
  even though the underlying painting is unambiguously PD.
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Madonna_in_a_Garland_of_Flowers_-_Peter_Paul_Rubens.jpg
- No genuine historical (pre-20th-c.) painting of an actual "May crowning" ceremony was found -
  that iconography lives mostly in 20th-century parish photos, not fine art.

## June - Month of the Sacred Heart

**Used:** Pompeo Batoni, "Sacred Heart of Jesus," 1767. CC0.
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:SacredHeartBatoni.jpg

Alternates:
- Corrado Giaquinto, "St. Margaret Mary Alacoque Contemplating the Sacred Heart of Jesus,"

- José de Páez, "Sacred Heart of Jesus with St. Ignatius of Loyola and St. Louis Gonzaga,"

## August - Month of the Immaculate Heart

**Used:** Anonymous, Immaculate Heart of Mary, 19th-century missal illustration. PD.
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Onbevlekthart.jpg

Alternates:
- Leopold Kupelwieser, Immaculate Heart of Mary, St. Peter's Church Vienna, 19th c. Flagged:
  Commons tags the photo CC BY-SA 4.0 rather than PD-Art even though the painting itself
  (artist died 1862) should qualify PD-old-100 - worth a license check before use.
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Immaculate_Heart_of_Mary.jpg
- José Ferreira Thedim, original 1920 statue of Our Lady of Fatima. Flagged: Commons marks it
  PD but the sculptor died 1971, so the PD rationale isn't a straightforward life+70/100 read -
  needs verification. Ties directly to the Fatima narrative if pursued.
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:First_Sculpture_of_Our_Lady_of_Fatima.jpg
- No genuinely old (pre-1920s) painting of the Fatima apparition scene itself exists in PD -
  the event is 1917, so period depictions are all 20th-century and mostly still ambiguous on
  the underlying artwork's copyright status.

## September - Month of Our Lady of Sorrows

**Used:** Carlo Dolci, "Mater Dolorosa," c. 1650. PD-old-100.
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Carlo_Dolci_Mater_dolorosa.jpg

Alternates:
- Murillo, "La Dolorosa," c. 1660-70 (Museo del Prado). PD-old-100.
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:La_Dolorosa_(Murillo).jpg
- Workshop of El Greco, "Mater Dolorosa," c. 1587-90 (Gemäldegalerie Berlin, more elongated/
  restrained style). PD-old-100.
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:El_Greco_-_Mater_Dolorosa_(Gemäldegalerie,_Berlin).jpg

All three are calm, bust-length "Mater Dolorosa" portraits (hands clasped, no graphic
Pietà/suffering imagery) per the site's not-dark guardrail.

## October - Month of the Holy Rosary

**Used:** Giovanni Battista Salvi da Sassoferrato, "Our Lady of the Rosary with Saint Dominic and
Saint Catherine," 17th century. PD-old-100.
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Our_Lady_of_the_Rosary_with_Saint_Dominic_and_Saint_Catherine).jpeg

Alternates:
- Caravaggio, "Madonna of the Rosary," c. 1607 (famous, but busier/darker tenebrist
  composition). PD-old-100.
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Madonna_of_the_Rosary-Caravaggio_(1607).jpg
- Bartolomeo Passarotti (workshop), "Portrait of Pope Pius V," c. 1566 (Walters Art Museum, PD).
  Good secondary/inline image if a Pius V portrait is wanted alongside the Marian art rather
  than instead of it.
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bartolomeo_Passarotti_-_Portrait_of_Pope_Pius_V_-_Walters_37453.jpg

## November - Month of the Holy Souls in Purgatory

**Used:** Anonymous, French School, "St. Odilo of Cluny" ("Odilo von Cluny zu Allerseelen"),
17th century. PD. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Odilo_von_Cluny_zu_Allerseelen_17Jh.jpg

Alternates:
- Jakub Schikaneder, "All Souls' Day," 1888 (dusk cemetery scene, figures laying flowers/
  wreaths - atmospheric and warm rather than grim, but more melancholic in tone than the Odilo
  portrait). PD-old-100 (artist died 1924). National Gallery Prague.
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jakub_Schikaneder_-_All_Souls%27_Day.jpg
- Victor Wolfvoet II (after Rubens), "Judas Maccabaeus Praying for the Dead," c. 1650 (the
  2 Maccabees scriptural basis for praying for the dead - a biblical-grounding option one step
  removed from the Odilo/confraternity narrative). PD-old-100.
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Peter_Paul_Rubens_(1577-1640)_(after)_-_Judas_Maccabaeus_Praying_for_the_Dead_-_446767_-_National_Trust.jpg
- Battistello Caracciolo, "Madonna delle anime purganti," c. 1625 (Capodimonte). Flagged: Commons
  shows the photo reproduction as CC-BY-SA 3.0, not a clean PD-Art tag - check before use even
  though the 1625 painting itself is long PD.
