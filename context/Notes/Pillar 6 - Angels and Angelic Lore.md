## Why this might be its own pillar

Pillars 1-5 are all anchored to saints, relics, or symbols. Angels aren't saints (no cultus, no
canonization), so folding Michael/Gabriel/Raphael stories into Pillar 1's "legendary biography
material" stretches that pillar's definition. A separate pillar also gives guardian-angel
folklore - which has no saint to anchor to at all - somewhere to live.

**Open question before confirming:** several entries below (the shrine-founding legends
especially) are nearly identical in shape to existing Pillar 1 entries. This may turn out to be
an `angels` tag/field layered onto Pillar 1 rather than a true 6th pillar - the same question
Pillar 5 faced before it split off from Pillar 1 (see that note's intro). Worth revisiting once
a few entries are drafted and it's clear whether they read as their own category or as Pillar 1
entries that happen to feature an angel instead of a saint.

## Categories

### Archangel legendary deeds

- **St. Michael at Monte Gargano** — the bull/cave founding legend (Italy), one of the oldest
  Michael shrine traditions in the West.
- **St. Michael at Mont-Saint-Michel** — the bishop Aubert vision (France), parallel shrine-
  founding shape to Monte Gargano above.
- **St. Michael's vision to Pope Gregory the Great** — atop what's now Castel Sant'Angelo in
  Rome, reportedly ending a plague; the castle's name and the bronze angel statue on top come
  from this legend.
- **Raphael in the Book of Tobit** — deuterocanonical, but canonical to Catholics, same
  "shared-origin material anchored in Catholic tradition" logic that lets Old Testament relic
  entries into Pillar 3.
- **Gabriel at the Annunciation** — told from the angelic-messenger angle rather than re-treading
  Marian ground that other sources already cover.

### Guardian angel traditions

- **Origin of the Oct. 2 feast day** and how it entered the liturgical calendar.
- **The "Angel of God" prayer** — history and spread as a devotional staple, especially for
  children.
- **Folk customs** — e.g. beliefs about which side of a person their angel walks on; needs a real
  source pass to confirm which cultures/regions these actually trace to before treating them as
  established folklore rather than invented.
- **Devotional art conventions** — why guardian angels are conventionally painted guiding children
  across bridges or dangerous terrain, and when that iconography emerged.

### Angelic interventions in saints' lives

- Angels reportedly bringing Communion to saints in prison or illness.
- Angelic choirs reportedly heard at a saint's death.
- Angels reportedly defending a besieged city or army — **flag for careful handling**: easy to
  drift into "miraculous battle" framing that reads closer to Register-coded verification
  territory than lore-coded storytelling. Keep the framing on "here's the story told about this,"
  not "here's evidence this happened."

### Angel-named or angel-founded shrines

- Mont-Saint-Michel, Monte Sant'Angelo, Castel Sant'Angelo, Our Lady of the Angels (Los Angeles'
  namesake). **Heavy overlap with Pillar 1's founding/shrine legends** — see the open schema
  question above; these might end up living in Pillar 1 tagged by place rather than requiring a
  separate pillar.

### How Catholic tradition organizes angels

- The nine choirs (per Pseudo-Dionysius) and why only three angels (Michael, Gabriel, Raphael)
  are given personal names in scripture. More reference/explainer than narrative — closer to the
  Pillar 5 symbols shape ("what it means, how usage developed") than a legend-with-retellings
  shape. May end up structured more like a symbols entry than a legends entry for this reason.

## Guardrail flags specific to this pillar

- **Spiritual-warfare / angel-vs-demon framing is out of scope.** The "not dark, no demonic
  content" rule excludes this directly — guardian-angel-protects-you-from-accident is fine,
  angel-battles-the-devil is not. Watch the archangel entries especially, since Michael is
  traditionally associated with that framing in a lot of popular material; keep the published
  version anchored to the shrine/apparition legend, not the warfare imagery.
- The besieged-city/army intervention entries (above) carry the same risk in a different shape -
  default to the softest, most devotional telling available rather than the most dramatic one.
